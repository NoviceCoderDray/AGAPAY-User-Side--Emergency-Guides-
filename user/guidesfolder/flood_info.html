A flood is an overflow of water onto land that is usually dry. Floods occur when water levels rise due to heavy rainfall, storm surges, overflowing rivers, or dam failures. Flooding can cause widespread damage to communities, infrastructure, and the environment.

Ang baha ay ang pag-apaw ng tubig sa mga lugar na karaniwang tuyo. Karaniwang dulot ito ng malakas na ulan, pag-apaw ng ilog, storm surge, o pagbagsak ng dam. Maaari itong magdulot ng pinsala sa mga tao at ari-arian.

Types of Floods

  • Flash Flood – Rapid flooding caused by heavy rainfall in a short period of time.
  • River Flood – Occurs when rivers overflow their banks due to continuous rainfall.
  • Coastal Flood – Caused by storm surges and high tides in coastal areas.
  • Urban Flood – Flooding in cities due to poor drainage and clogged waterways.
  • Dam/Levee Failure Flood – Sudden release of large volumes of water when barriers collapse.

Effects of Floods

  • Loss of lives and displacement of families
  • Damage to houses, crops, and infrastructure
  • Spread of waterborne diseases (e.g., leptospirosis, cholera)
  • Disruption of transportation and communication systems
  • Economic losses and long-term recovery challenges

Rainfall Warning (PAGASA Color-Coded System)

  • Yellow Warning – Indicates moderate to heavy rainfall. Flash flooding may occur in low-lying and poorly-drained areas.
  • Orange Warning – Signifies intense, prolonged rainfall. Flooding is likely, especially in vulnerable areas.
  • Red Warning – Extreme rainfall expected. Severe flooding and possible landslides; move to higher ground immediately.

These warnings are issued by PAGASA to help the public prepare and respond appropriately depending on the severity of rainfall.

Did You Know?

The Philippines is highly prone to floods due to its tropical climate and frequent typhoons. Metro Manila and low-lying areas in Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao often experience seasonal flooding during the rainy season.
