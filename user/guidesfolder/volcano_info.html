A volcano is an opening in the Earth’s crust where molten rock (magma), gases, and ash escape to the surface. When pressure builds up inside the Earth, a volcanic eruption occurs, releasing lava, ash clouds, and pyroclastic flows.

Ang bulkan ay isang siwang sa balat ng mundo kung saan lumalabas ang tunaw na bato (magma), abo, at gas. Kapag naipon ang matinding presyon, nagaganap ang pagsabog ng bulkan.

Types of Volcanoes

  • Shield Volcano – Broad, gently sloping sides built by fluid lava flows (e.g., Jolo Volcanic Complex, Sulu).
  • Composite/Stratovolcano – Steep-sided, layered volcanoes with explosive eruptions (e.g., Mayon Volcano, Albay).
  • Cinder Cone – Small, steep volcanoes formed from ash, cinders, and volcanic rocks (e.g., Mt. Iraya, Batanes).
  • Caldera – Large depression formed when a volcano collapses after a massive eruption (e.g., Taal Volcano, Batangas).
  • Lava Dome – Dome-shaped mound formed by slow extrusion of thick, viscous lava (e.g., Mt. Bulusan Lava Dome, Sorsogon).
  • Mid-Ocean Ridges – Underwater volcanic mountain chains formed by seafloor spreading (e.g., Philippine Sea Ridges).

Types of Eruptions

  • Hawaiian – Gentle eruptions with highly fluid lava fountains and flows.
  • Strombolian – Moderate bursts of lava, ash, and volcanic bombs, usually short-lived.
  • Vulcanian – Short, violent explosions ejecting ash, gas, and rock fragments.
  • Pelean – Characterized by pyroclastic flows caused by dome collapse or explosion.
  • Plinian – Extremely powerful eruptions producing tall ash columns, pumice, and widespread fallout.
  • Phreatic – Steam-driven explosions caused by water interacting with hot magma or rock.
  • Effusive – Gentle lava flow forming broad layers over time.
  • Explosive – Violent eruptions releasing ash clouds, pyroclastic flows, and gas.

PHIVOLCS Volcano Alert Levels

  • Alert Level 0 – Normal : No eruption in foreseeable future. Safe condition.
  • Alert Level 1 – Low-Level Unrest : Slight increase in seismic and volcanic activity; no eruption imminent.
  • Alert Level 2 – Moderate Unrest : Increasing unrest; eruption possible within weeks to months.
  • Alert Level 3 – Relatively High Unrest : Intrusion of magma at the crater; eruption possible within days or weeks.
  • Alert Level 4 – Intense Unrest : Hazardous eruption possible within days.
  • Alert Level 5 – Hazardous Eruption Ongoing : Hazardous eruption is in progress.

Effects of Volcanic Eruptions

  • Ashfall damaging health, crops, and infrastructure
  • Lava flows destroying property and land
  • Pyroclastic flows causing extreme hazards
  • Lahars (mudflows) burying communities
  • Climate effects from volcanic gases and ash

Did You Know?

The Philippines is part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, home to over 20 active volcanoes. The eruption of Mt. Pinatubo in 1991 was one of the largest of the 20th century, affecting global climate for years.
