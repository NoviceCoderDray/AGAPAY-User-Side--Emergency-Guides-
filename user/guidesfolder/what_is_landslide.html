A landslide is the movement of rock, soil, and debris down a slope due to gravity. It can be triggered by natural factors like heavy rain, earthquakes, and volcanic activity, or by human activities such as deforestation and mining. Landslides can occur suddenly or develop slowly over time.

Common Causes

  • Heavy and prolonged rainfall saturating the soil
  • Earthquakes shaking unstable slopes
  • Volcanic eruptions producing lahars or ash flows
  • Human activities such as road construction and quarrying
  • Deforestation removing slope protection

Types of Landslides

  • Rotational Landslide – movement along a curved surface causing backward rotation of the slide mass.
  • Translational Landslide – slides along flat surfaces, minimal rotation.
  • Block Slide – a coherent block sliding downslope as one unit.
  • Rockfall – sudden falling, bouncing, or rolling of rock from cliffs.
  • Topple – forward rotation of material about a pivot at the base.
  • Debris Flow – fast-moving slurry of water, rock, and soil.
  • Debris Avalanche – extremely rapid, destructive debris flow.
  • Earth Flow – viscous movement of fine-grained, water-saturated soil.
  • Creep – imperceptibly slow downward movement of soil or rock.
  • Lateral Spread – horizontal extension of material often due to liquefaction.

Slow vs Fast Landslides

  • Slow – happens over weeks or months; often gives warning signs such as cracks or leaning trees.
  • Fast – happens in seconds or minutes; highly dangerous with little or no warning.

Areas at Risk in the Philippines

  • Mountainous regions like Benguet, Ifugao, and Mountain Province
  • Eastern Visayas, especially Leyte and Samar
  • Upland Mindanao, especially Bukidnon and Davao regions
  • Coastal cliffs and steep road cuts

Effects of Landslides

  • Loss of life and destruction of homes
  • Damage to roads, bridges, and infrastructure
  • Burial of farmland and crops
  • Blocked rivers causing floods

Did You Know?

Landslides are the most frequent secondary hazard after typhoons in the Philippines. Even moderate rain can cause landslides if the soil is already saturated.
