A typhoon is a powerful tropical cyclone that forms over the Northwest Pacific Ocean. It is characterized by strong winds, heavy rain, and storm surges. In the Philippines, typhoons are called “bagyo” and occur frequently due to the country’s location in the Pacific typhoon belt.

Ang bagyo ay isang malakas na unos na nabubuo sa Karagatang Pasipiko. Ito ay may dalang malalakas na hangin, matinding ulan, at storm surge. Madalas itong maranasan sa Pilipinas dahil sa kinalalagyan nito sa Pacific typhoon belt.

How Typhoons Form

  • Warm ocean waters (at least 26°C) provide energy.
  • Moist air rises and condenses, releasing heat.
  • Rotation of the Earth (Coriolis effect) causes spiraling winds.
  • Low-pressure center forms the “eye” of the typhoon.

Categories of Typhoons (Philippines)

  • Tropical Depression – Winds of up to 61 km/h.
  • Tropical Storm – Winds of 62–88 km/h.
  • Severe Tropical Storm – Winds of 89–117 km/h.
  • Typhoon – Winds of 118–220 km/h.
  • Super Typhoon – Winds exceeding 220 km/h.

Effects of Typhoons

  • Flooding in low-lying and coastal areas
  • Landslides in mountainous regions
  • Storm surges damaging coastal communities
  • Power outages and infrastructure damage
  • Loss of lives and livelihoods

Did You Know?

The Philippines experiences about 20 typhoons every year, with around 5–7 being destructive. Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda) in 2013 was one of the strongest recorded, with winds exceeding 300 km/h.
